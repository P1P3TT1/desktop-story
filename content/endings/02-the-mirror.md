You don't write anything else. You lock the door, then check it twice. You turn off every light except the one in the bedroom.

1:30 AM comes and goes. Somewhere across the city, someone in a red scarf waits on an empty pier, and then stops waiting.

At 3:33 AM you sit down in front of the mirror, just like S. told you to.

At first there is only you. Tired eyes. The small scar above your left eyebrow.

Then there are two of you.

One of them blinks when you don't.

"Thank you for staying," she says. Her mouth moves. Yours doesn't. "I couldn't do this if you had gone. It only works while you're looking."

The glass is warm under your fingertips. Then it isn't glass at all.

* * *

On Saturday morning, Sarah Chen calls her mother for the first time in three weeks.

"You sound different, sweetheart," Mom says.

"I've just been tired." She pours herself a cup of coffee and signs the delivery slip on the counter with her right hand. "Everything's fine now. Really."

In the bedroom, the mirror fogs up from the inside.
